Goal #1 · Establish the system type, then read and translate if warranted

We are trying to establish what rongorongo encodes.
No defensible reading or translation exists yet.

Our destination is a defensible English translation if the evidence supports one. First we must canonicalize the corpus, determine whether it is full writing or another system, and recover defensible source-language or source-system readings. We document the evidence, failures and permissions.

Wins so far · in plain English

What we have actually accomplished

Barthel's own 1958 text has now replaced several misleading headline totals with a documented counting scheme. This is corpus groundwork, not a glyph reading or translation.

Parity-audit limit: A third-party 15/15 comparison checks only one Aa01 sample line; it does not establish whole-corpus equivalence. No corpus bytes were accessed, and written rights remain unresolved.

Corpus and rights note: Kohaumotu's digitized-corpus page describes CEIPP's Barthel numerical transliteration as available to bona fide researchers. The legacy CEIPP notice permits non-profit copying of that site's contents with acknowledgment, while the newer copyright page says underlying Barthel/Fischer materials remain copyrighted and are used under a fair-use assumption. This is an access lead, not blanket permission for bulk retrieval, derivatives or publication. No corpus was downloaded.

Parity-audit caveat: the third-party “15/15” check compares only the Aa01 sample line with one prefix of one reformatted entry; it does not establish whole-corpus parity. See the pinned script. No corpus bytes were accessed, and rights remain unresolved.

Corpus-rights and parity limit: a third-party 15/15 check covers one Aa01 sample line only, not the whole corpus. No CEIPP XML or tablet corpus was accessed; written permission for the exact files and permitted uses is still missing.

01

Barthel used 603 occupied slots in a range of 799

A direct read of Barthel's 1958 catalog reports 799 reserved positions, 603 occupied positions, and 322 signs occurring at least three times. Catalog numbers 632 and 638 are real glyph identifiers, but that fact alone does not prove how later secondary sources produced their conflicting totals. No glyph meaning was recovered.
