# Prior-claims catalog

Directly modeled on the sibling Oak Island and Phaistos Disc projects' own prior-claims catalogs: a
structured, citable record of claimed readings and interpretive disputes in rongorongo scholarship, so
this project doesn't retread already-examined ground. First entry below converts research already done
(`logs/2026-10-10-sq1-sq4-guy1990-alternate-route-priority-dispute.md`) into this catalog's proper home.

## The Guy (1990) / Rjabchikov (1989) priority dispute over the Mamari lunar-calendar reading

**The claim being disputed**: Jacques B. M. Guy's "The Lunar Calendar of Tablet Mamari" (*Journal de la
Société des Océanistes* 91(2), 1990, pp. 135–149) is the most widely cited reading of the Mamari tablet's
lunar-calendar sequence — described in secondary literature as holding "the only text whose general
meaning is known beyond reasonable doubt" (per a catalogue-abstract summary, not yet independently
confirmed at primary-source tier).

**The dispute**: directly read (PDF-extraction, not search snippet) in Sergei V. Rjabchikov's own arXiv
paper, part of his "The Ancient Astronomy of Easter Island: The Mamari Tablet Tells" series: "Guy (1990)
as well as Kelley and Milone (2011: 339–340, figure 11.1) repeated basic ideas of my decipherment of the
Mamari calendar record (Rjabchikov 1989: 123–125, figures 2 and 3)." This is an explicit priority claim —
Rjabchikov asserting his own 1989 work predates and was the real source of ideas Guy's more widely-cited
1990 paper is credited with.

**Evidentiary status, disclosed plainly**: this is **Rjabchikov's own claim about himself**, read directly
from his own paper — not an independently adjudicated priority finding. No response from Guy, no
third-party assessment of the dispute, and no read of Rjabchikov's actual 1989 source material have been
found or sought yet. Per this project's own standing discipline toward independent/self-published
rongorongo claims, this is recorded as a disclosed, named dispute — not validated as fact, and not a
reason to prefer either reading over the other.

**Why this matters for SQ-1/SQ-4**: if this project later obtains Guy's actual glyph-to-night
correspondence table (SQ-1's own standing blocker), it would be worth checking it against Rjabchikov's
1989 claim too, not just treating Guy's reading as uncontested.

## "Lines to Sing: The Complete Acoustic Decipherment of Rongorongo" (Rios Jr., 2026) — self-published, independent

**The claim**: directly fetched, primary tier, from the paper's own Zenodo archival record (DOI
`10.5281/zenodo.19140709`, published 2026-03-21): author Abelardo Rios Jr., listed as an "independent
researcher" with no institutional affiliation, explicitly labeled a "working paper" with no indication of
peer review. The paper argues rongorongo is "not a linguistic writing system but a functional acoustic
notation system" — a chanting-performance guide encoding frequency, amplitude, and harmonic instructions,
supported by sign-morphology analysis, Helmholtz resonance calculations on inscribed objects, and a
comparison to the Marshall Islands' mattang stick charts (an independently documented wayfinding-notation
system, used here only as an analogical reference point, not independently re-verified this cycle). The
abstract states five falsifiable predictions — not independently checked against those predictions this
cycle.

**Two independently fetched sources, one minor discrepancy disclosed rather than silently resolved**: the
hosting page at `synapsesocial.com/papers/69bf899af665edcd009e95c1` lists a "+1" co-author not named on
the Zenodo record itself (which names only Rios Jr. as "Researcher"), and gives a slightly different date
(March 22, 2026) than Zenodo's own metadata (published March 21, 2026 v1; created March 20, 2026; last
modified May 13, 2026). Both discrepancies are left open rather than guessed at — Zenodo is treated as the
more authoritative record of the two since it is the paper's own archival deposit, but the co-author
discrepancy in particular is unresolved.

**Evidentiary status, disclosed plainly**: directly-fetched-tier for title, author, claim, and metadata —
not a reading of the full text or the five stated predictions. Per this project's own standing discipline
toward independent/self-published claims (matching the sibling Phaistos Disc and Oak Island projects'
treatment of similar sources), this is recorded as a disclosed, citable claim — **not validated, not
relied upon as evidence about rongorongo's actual nature**, regardless of how specific or
methodologically dressed-up the framing is. This resolves the vague placeholder below: this is one of the
"several self-published fully deciphered claims" an earlier cycle noted without a specific citation.

## Steven Roger Fischer's "triad" decipherment claim (1995) — the most prominent historical claim, five named rebuttals

**The claim**: in 1995, independent linguist Steven R. Fischer announced he had deciphered
rongorongo's Santiago Staff (a 2,320-glyph text). His central example (the sequence "606.76 700
8," roughly halfway through line 12) reads glyph 76 as a phallus, interpreting a recurring
"X-phallus Y Z" pattern as "X copulated with Y, there issued forth Z," and glyph 606 as "all the
birds" via a claimed semantic shift from "hand" to a plural marker — rendering the passage as a
creation chant about birds, fish, and the sun.

**Five specific, independently-sourced rebuttals, directly fetched from Wikipedia's dedicated
"Decipherment of rongorongo" article**:
1. Andrew Robinson's structural count: only 63 of the staff's sequences fully fit the claimed
   triad pattern, the maximum even with generous counting.
2. Pozdniakov and Pozdniakov's frequency argument: the four glyphs in Fischer's own main example
   make up ~20% of the entire corpus — common enough that the same reading logic would produce
   absurd results elsewhere in the text.
3. Jacques Guy's linguistic objection: the word Fischer reads as a Rapanui plural marker (*mau*)
   is a Tahitian grammatical element that functions as a post-nominal adjective ("true,
   genuine") in Polynesian languages generally, not a plural marker.
4. Guy's second objection: Fischer's supporting parallel text (*Atua Matariri*) relies on an
   English translation that doesn't match its own source's Rapanui transcription; a separate,
   independent reading of the same text mentions neither birds nor fish.
5. Richard Sproat's computational test: automated string-matching shows the Santiago Staff
   stands apart from the rest of the corpus on its own terms, and removing the "phallus" glyph
   central to Fischer's method from the analysis doesn't change that result — undermining the
   specific assumption his reading method depended on.

**Why this entry is stronger than the two above**: five independent critics, each with a
different kind of objection (structural, statistical, linguistic, textual, computational),
converge on rejecting the same claim from five separate angles — the wider scholarly consensus,
per the same source, is that Fischer's readings came "not accompanied by the least
justification" and do not generalize to other texts.

**Evidentiary status, disclosed plainly**: Wikipedia-article tier — a well-sourced secondary
synthesis, not yet a direct read of Fischer's own publications or of any of the five named
critics' own original papers. Each is a concrete, citable lead for a future primary-source
upgrade pass, not attempted this cycle. The source article itself carries an internal `[sic]` on
Robinson's quoted sequence-count figure, disclosed here rather than silently corrected.
