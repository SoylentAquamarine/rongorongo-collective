# Prior-claims catalog

Directly modeled on the sibling Oak Island and Phaistos Disc projects' own prior-claims catalogs: a
structured, citable record of claimed readings and interpretive disputes in rongorongo scholarship, so
this project doesn't retread already-examined ground. First entry below converts research already done
(`logs/2026-10-10-sq1-sq4-guy1990-alternate-route-priority-dispute.md`) into this catalog's proper home.

## The Guy (1990) / Rjabchikov (1989) priority dispute over the Mamari lunar-calendar reading

**The claim being disputed**: Jacques B. M. Guy's "The Lunar Calendar of Tablet Mamari" (*Journal de la
Société des Océanistes* 91(2), 1990, pp. 135–149) is the most widely cited reading of the Mamari tablet's
lunar-calendar sequence — described in secondary literature as holding "the only text whose general
meaning is known beyond reasonable doubt" (per a catalogue-abstract summary, not yet independently
confirmed at primary-source tier).

**The dispute**: directly read (PDF-extraction, not search snippet) in Sergei V. Rjabchikov's own arXiv
paper, part of his "The Ancient Astronomy of Easter Island: The Mamari Tablet Tells" series: "Guy (1990)
as well as Kelley and Milone (2011: 339–340, figure 11.1) repeated basic ideas of my decipherment of the
Mamari calendar record (Rjabchikov 1989: 123–125, figures 2 and 3)." This is an explicit priority claim —
Rjabchikov asserting his own 1989 work predates and was the real source of ideas Guy's more widely-cited
1990 paper is credited with.

**Evidentiary status, disclosed plainly**: this is **Rjabchikov's own claim about himself**, read directly
from his own paper — not an independently adjudicated priority finding. No response from Guy, no
third-party assessment of the dispute, and no read of Rjabchikov's actual 1989 source material have been
found or sought yet. Per this project's own standing discipline toward independent/self-published
rongorongo claims, this is recorded as a disclosed, named dispute — not validated as fact, and not a
reason to prefer either reading over the other.

**Why this matters for SQ-1/SQ-4**: if this project later obtains Guy's actual glyph-to-night
correspondence table (SQ-1's own standing blocker), it would be worth checking it against Rjabchikov's
1989 claim too, not just treating Guy's reading as uncontested.
